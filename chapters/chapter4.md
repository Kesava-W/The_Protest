**Chapter 4: The Informer Revealed**

**Scene 1 – The Hospital Reunion**

The rhythmic beeping of the cardiac monitor blended with the muted bustle of the oncology ward, the smell of antiseptic lingering in the air the way it always did by late afternoon.

Vivaswa sat beside his father's bed, gently massaging Vashista's feet. The chemotherapy session had left the old man exhausted; his eyes were closed, though he wasn't asleep. Every now and then his face tightened ever so slightly before relaxing again, and Vivaswa noticed every time.

"You don't have to hide it from me, Nanna," he said softly, without looking up.

Vashista opened his eyes and smiled faintly. "If I complain, you'll stop smiling."

"I wasn't smiling."

"You were." Vivaswa finally looked up, and the old man chuckled weakly. "You've always thought you could fool everyone. Never me."

Vivaswa shook his head with a reluctant smile and continued pressing his father's feet with careful hands.

"I'm fine," Vashista said.

"I know."

"No." The old man looked straight at him. "You know that I'm saying it for you."

Silence settled between them. Outside the room, a trolley rolled past, someone called for a nurse, and a child cried somewhere down the corridor before being comforted. Life continued, as it always did in hospitals, in the background of everything.

Vivaswa adjusted the blanket around his father's legs. "The doctor said today's session went better than last week's."

"Hmm."

"You heard him."

"I heard him."

"But?"

Vashista smiled knowingly. "You've started answering questions with questions."

Vivaswa didn't respond. His father studied him for a moment. "You've changed."

The words weren't accusatory — just observant, the way Vashista observed everything.

"Life changes people," Vivaswa said quietly.

"It shouldn't change your heart."

Vivaswa stopped massaging for a brief second before resuming. "It hasn't."

Vashista placed a frail hand on his son's shoulder. "I hope not."

A gentle knock came from the open door. Both men looked up. Sandhya stood outside, dressed in simple formal clothes rather than her usual field attire, her press identity card hanging from her neck. Beside her stood a man in plain clothes — tall, athletic, calm, his sharp eyes scanning the room in a single practiced glance before resting on Vivaswa.

Sandhya smiled. "May we come in?"

Vivaswa immediately began to rise. The stranger raised a hand. "No. Please remain seated." His voice was composed, used to being obeyed without needing to be raised.

Vivaswa hesitated but stayed put. The man walked inside, stopped beside Sandhya, and looked at Vashista first. "How are you feeling today, sir?"

"A little better."

"Good." He nodded respectfully before turning toward Vivaswa. For a few seconds neither man spoke. Then the stranger's expression softened.

"I've wanted to meet you properly."

Vivaswa said nothing. Sandhya looked between them, then smiled — the smile of someone who had been waiting weeks for exactly this moment.

"Vivaswa… I'd like you to meet ACP Satvik."

Vivaswa looked at the officer. Satvik extended his hand. "This meeting is long overdue."

Vivaswa stood this time. The two men shook hands, and Satvik held it a moment longer than usual, unmistakable respect in his eyes. "Thank you," he said simply.

Vivaswa frowned slightly. "For what?"

Satvik smiled. "We'll come to that."

The room fell silent again. Vashista looked curiously between the three of them, sensing — correctly — that a conversation had begun long before this meeting, and that he was only now being allowed to see its final chapter.

**Scene 2 – The Police Connection**

The rhythmic beeping of the cardiac monitor filled the room. Vashista had drifted into a light sleep after the physiotherapy session, one leg resting on the bed with the support of a pillow, the IV line emptying slowly into his arm.

Vivaswa remained seated beside him. ACP Satvik stood near the foot of the bed, watching him for a few moments before speaking.

"You know," he said with a faint smile, "I've handled informers for years. They usually exaggerate. They panic, jump to conclusions, or give information after everything has already happened." A brief pause. "You did none of that."

Sandhya watched both of them quietly.

"Your information was timely," Satvik continued, looking directly at Vivaswa. "Almost every update you passed on was accurate. Not perfect." A slight smile. "But accurate enough that we could verify the remaining pieces ourselves."

Vivaswa lowered his eyes. "I only told you what I saw."

"I know. And that is exactly why it was valuable."

Silence settled for a moment before Sandhya spoke. "I wasn't sure he'd agree." Vivaswa looked toward her, and she smiled faintly. "The day you told me about Uncle's illness—" her eyes shifted toward Vashista, "—I knew you wouldn't accept charity. So I spoke to Sir."

Satvik picked up the thread. "Sandhya has helped us on several investigations over the years." He looked at her with quiet pride. "She trusts facts more than rumours." Turning back to Vivaswa, he added, "She told me about your situation. I met you. I verified what you had observed. And after that, we made an arrangement."

Vivaswa remained silent.

"You helped us understand what was happening inside the protest." Satvik's tone stayed professional. "In return, I ensured your father's treatment would continue."

Vivaswa glanced toward his sleeping father, his eyes lingering there for several seconds before he finally spoke. "Thank you." The words were quiet, but they carried far more weight than their volume suggested.

Satvik waved the gratitude away. "You earned it. No one works for free."

Another silence. Satvik walked slowly toward the window. "You know," he said, turning back, "you don't behave like a data entry operator. You observe routines. You notice patterns. You remember faces. You separate facts from assumptions." A faint smile crossed his face. "Those qualities are difficult to teach."

Sandhya smiled knowingly. "I've been telling him that for years."

Satvik nodded. "I've already spoken to a few people." Vivaswa looked surprised. "When the time comes, you should apply." He let the sentence hang deliberately. "I can't recruit anyone directly. But I can certainly recommend capable people. And if everything checks out, I'll make sure your application reaches the right desks."

Vivaswa stared at him. "You really think I can do it?"

"I think you'd make a good investigator," Satvik said without hesitation.

A faint smile appeared on Vivaswa's face for the first time that day — not because of the job itself, but because after weeks of helplessness, someone had finally looked at him and seen ability instead of misfortune.

Satvik noticed the expression. "Don't thank me yet. The recommendation only opens a door. You'll still have to walk through it yourself."

"I understand."

"I expected that answer."

The room grew quiet again. Outside, footsteps echoed through the hospital corridor. Inside, only the monitor continued its steady rhythm. After a thoughtful pause, Satvik looked at Vivaswa once more, his expression shifting into something more serious.

"There is one more thing you should know."

Vivaswa slowly raised his head. The room fell completely silent.

**Scene 3 – Fates Decided**

"We finally identified the people who ran over your father."

For the first time since Satvik had entered the room, Vivaswa allowed a flicker of surprise to cross his face. "You… found them?"

Satvik nodded. "Yes." A brief silence. "It was Manish."

Vivaswa's eyes narrowed almost imperceptibly.

"And the man driving with him," Satvik continued, "was Suresh."

Sandhya went very still. The same Suresh, she registered dimly, whose funding trail she'd spent weeks failing to prove. She turned instinctively toward Vivaswa. He remained seated beside his father, his expression unreadable, and after a few seconds he asked quietly, "Are you certain?"

"The CCTV footage from a nearby junction, vehicle registration records, and subsequent corroboration left very little room for doubt." Satvik paused. "They didn't stop after the collision."

The words hung heavily in the room. Vashista closed his eyes for a brief moment but said nothing.

"There is something else." Satvik looked directly at Vivaswa. "Manish won't be facing life the way he imagined. He suffered severe neurological damage — the entire left side of his body is paralysed." His tone stayed clinical, almost apologetic about its own precision. "The doctors called it unusual — heavier and more rapid damage than they'd expect on one side alone — but the tests were conclusive. Chronic arsenic exposure, likely inhaled. They haven't traced the source. They may never."

Sandhya looked shocked. "When?"

"Recently. The doctors are still evaluating the long-term prognosis."

Another silence settled. Vivaswa lowered his eyes. "I see." Nothing more. No satisfaction. No anger. Just two quiet words.

Satvik observed him carefully. Most people, hearing news like this, reacted instantly — some smiled, some cursed, some celebrated. Vivaswa did none of them, and Satvik couldn't quite decide whether that restraint impressed him or unsettled him.

He shifted the conversation. "The same Suresh who was driving that morning—" Vivaswa's stomach turned cold, "—is dead."

Sandhya's hand went to her mouth. "He died three days ago while travelling to Mumbai. A highway accident."

For the first time, Vivaswa spoke immediately. "How?"

"Brake failure." Satvik answered without hesitation. "The forensic report confirmed it. There wasn't any foul play. His vehicle lost control on a downhill stretch. It was over before emergency services reached him."

The room fell silent once more. Satvik glanced at his watch. "I should leave." He turned to Vashista. "I hope your recovery continues well."

"Thank you, Officer," Vashista said, smiling faintly.

Satvik nodded respectfully, then looked at Vivaswa. "Think seriously about what I said. The department needs people who observe before they speak. And people who remain calm when everyone else loses their head."

Vivaswa stood. "I will."

Satvik gave Sandhya a brief nod. "I'll see you outside." He walked to the door, and it closed softly behind him.

Silence returned. Only after several long seconds did Vivaswa speak, almost to himself. "So close…"

Sandhya looked at him. He kept staring at the closed door.

"I wish I'd reached Suresh first."

His voice carried no frustration — only quiet regret. Sandhya said nothing. She simply looked at him, unable to tell whether the man beside her had just found closure, or whether something far darker had been quietly buried beneath that calm exterior.
